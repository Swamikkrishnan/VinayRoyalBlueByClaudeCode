My Path

I feel at home in life.

Peaceful. Grounded. Dynamic.

I still meet blind spots and overwhelm, but I trust my capacity to move through them.

I wasn’t always this way.

For a long time, I coped by making my world smaller.

Read more: I wasn’t always this way

I escaped into games, drugs and whatever helped me avoid what I did not know how to meet.

Dishonesty became a reflex. Discipline was difficult. So were relationships, commitments and ordinary responsibilities.

Outside the environments I could control, everyday situations could overwhelm me and become crises.

By 24, I had dropped out of university, had almost no trust in myself, and felt helpless and hopeless.

I knew what mattered. I wanted change.

I still could not follow through.

The turning point

I began learning how to welcome more of life.

Read more: The turning point

I returned to pranayama and meditation, practices I had known from childhood and later dismissed.

One morning, I woke with a clear sense that life was beautiful and that I still had a place in it.

That experience opened the door into years of healing, personal development and spiritual practice.

The real practice

Healing spaces opened the door. Ordinary life taught me how to stay open.

Read more: The real practice

Relationships, responsibility and everyday commitments took the work further.

I had to learn to stay when things became difficult. Tell the truth. Keep my word. Repair harm. Show up when I wanted to disappear.

The challenges grew in scale and consequence.

So did my capacity to meet them.

As my capacity to meet life grew, so did the scale of what I felt called to serve.

My Vision

A world where power serves Life.

The wider dream

Enough people in leadership who are willing to face what is difficult, within themselves and within the systems they lead.

That changes how power is held.

Before acting, we ask:

What does this serve?
Whom will it affect?
What will it cost?

What we refuse to face does not disappear. Its cost is carried somewhere, often by other people, other species or those who come after us.

As the frame widens, so does responsibility.

Human lives. Other species. Ecosystems. Future generations.
