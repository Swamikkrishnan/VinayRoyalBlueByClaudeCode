The Approach

Welcoming all of life, embodied.

Welcoming all of life has a tangible expression: vitality in the body, healthy immune and hormonal function, and a natural capacity for peace, warmth, care and connection.

It also looks like a resilient, antifragile nervous system — steadiness under pressure, quicker recovery, and a growing capacity through challenge.

To understand how I work towards this, it helps to look more closely at life itself — through interconnection and movement.

One

Life is interconnected

One of the clearest places to observe life is the living human body.

  • The lungs bring in oxygen.
  • The heart circulates it.
  • Digestion provides energy.
  • The nervous system coordinates how we respond.

Each part has its own role — together, they sustain the whole.

When one is strained, the others compensate. If the strain continues, the whole person is affected. The body lives through this interdependence.

Each part has its own role. Together, they sustain one continuous whole — and strain in any one is felt by all the others.

Two

Life moves

A plant grows. A river flows. The body breathes, digests, repairs, adapts and rests.

When life is working well, things can move when and where they need to.

When that movement becomes restricted, it may show up as —

stuckness · procrastination · low energy · burnout · indecision · overthinking · emotional overwhelm · difficulty following through · loss of direction · relationship troubles · the sense that things should be improving, but somehow aren’t

At other times, there is no particular restriction. Life is already moving, and the work is to develop more strength, range or capacity for what comes next.

The Wheel of Life

A simple way to see interconnection and movement together

Consider your life as a wheel. Its different areas form the spokes, and every spoke contributes to the movement of the whole.

A wheel can continue rolling with a weakened spoke, but the others begin carrying more of the load. Over time it wears unevenly and becomes less stable. Our lives work in much the same way.

Poor sleep can reduce energy, concentration and patience, making work and relationships harder. Improving sleep can create movement across all three areas.

Strain travels across the wheel. So can recovery.

The spokes show us where in life something is happening.

Within any spoke, I have found four places where we can work directly to create or restore movement.

Body

The physical body itself. Movement here may come through exertion, settling, release, recovery or rest.

sleepnutritionmovementstrengthimmunityinjurytensionrecovery

Mind

How we perceive, interpret and make sense of what is happening. Sometimes we need to think something through, see it differently, or give the mind enough space to process what has happened.

beliefsassumptionsclarityconfusionperspectivereflection

Emotion

A distinct layer of human experience to address. Sometimes an emotion needs enough safety to be fully felt; sometimes what has been held back needs to move. When the process completes, perspective can shift and a different response becomes possible.

fearangergriefshameguiltjealousyjoylovegratitude

Action

How inner change enters the rest of life. Sometimes there is nothing more to understand or feel — a call needs to be made, a boundary set, a habit changed, a goal given a plan. Something needs to happen.

relationshipsworkmoneygoalscommitmentshabitsplansboundariesdisciplinefollow-through

Finding where to begin

There is no fixed order.

A shift in the body may release emotion. Feeling an emotion may change the mind. Clarity may make action possible. Action may change all three.

The pattern is different for every person. We begin by understanding where the imbalance is showing, then look across Body, Mind, Emotion and Action for the most useful place to work.

What needs attention now for the next movement to happen?

We begin there, allow the change to integrate, and then look again. What has shifted helps reveal the next step.

The FACE framework

A practice for welcoming more of life

Where do we get caught in an old pattern, react, shut down or struggle to stay with what is happening? FACE is a framework for meeting that edge.

Choose a letter to open it.

F Focus

Focus on what keeps repeating.

A thought that keeps looping. Someone or something that reliably sets you off. A pattern of tension in the body. A behaviour you repeat even when you do not want to. Something you want to do but cannot seem to begin.

You may notice it while it is happening, or only later.

You cannot change what you cannot see.

A Admit

Admit where you are not free yet.

Seeing a pattern does not mean you can immediately stop it. Be honest about where it overrides your intentions, narrows your choices or keeps costing you.

Notice where you minimise it, justify it or mistake understanding it for being free of it. If you can see what is driving it, admit that too. If not, begin with exactly what you know.

Freedom begins with admitting where you are not free.

C Connect

Connect with what is underneath.

There may be a feeling, memory, body response, fear, need, truth or practical reality that has been difficult to meet. Return to it from a steadier place — sometimes alone, sometimes with another person or skilled support to help you stay.

When you can remain with it without becoming overwhelmed in the same way, its hold can begin to loosen. You may feel what you could not feel, see what you could not see, or respond in a way that was not available before.

Protection becomes limitation when it outlives the danger.

E Embody

Embody a different response.

Practise what was not available before. Slow down where you would rush. Pause instead of reacting. Speak where you would usually stay silent. Set the boundary you would normally let slide. Act where you would usually freeze.

At first, this may take effort. The old response has simply had more practice.

Practice turns insight into instinct.

Taken together, FACE is a way of moving from being caught in a pattern toward having more choice in how you respond.

Focus on what keeps repeating · Admit where you are not free yet · Connect with what is underneath, with enough steadiness to stay · Embody a different response.

FACE is a way to orient when you feel caught and know something needs attention. It isn’t a system for constantly monitoring or correcting yourself.

Some things shift quickly. Others ask for attention, repetition or time.

A pattern returning does not mean the progress is lost.

Honour your pace. Lean in, ease off, pause or get help. There is no rush and no falling behind. With practice, you begin to notice the flinch sooner, regain your footing more quickly, and carry on without every difficult moment becoming a crisis.

The more we face, the more we can embrace.
