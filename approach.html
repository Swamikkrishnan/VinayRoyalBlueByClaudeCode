The Approach

Why welcome?

When we feel welcome, we relax. We open up.

The same principle can shape how we relate to what comes.

From bracing against life to embracing it.

Welcoming life means growing our capacity to meet more of what it brings.

Read more

Some parts of our experience are easy to welcome. Others make something in us pull back.

  • Money
  • Trust
  • Relationships
  • Conflict
  • Sex
  • Being seen
  • Change
  • Nature
  • The unknown

Sometimes we notice it. Sometimes we don’t.

As our capacity to welcome life expands, there is less to fear, tolerate or simply get through, and more to enter, experience and enjoy.

More of its depth, beauty, intensity, stillness and mystery becomes available to us.

What this looks like in the body

We can stay steadier under pressure and recover more easily.

Read more
  • A nervous system with more flexibility, resilience and room to respond under pressure.
  • Greater vitality and stronger immunity.
  • More emotional breadth, with greater access to peace, warmth, care, connection and choice.

So how do we work towards this?

Two principles give us a useful starting point.

Everything is connected

Nothing in us exists in isolation. A shift in one part can affect the whole.

Read more

One of the clearest places to see this is the living human body.

  • The lungs bring in oxygen.
  • The heart circulates it.
  • Digestion provides energy.
  • The nervous system coordinates how we respond.

Each part has its own role. Together, they sustain the whole.

When one is strained, the others adapt.

Movement is natural

Health depends on movement: adapting, responding, recovering and changing when needed.

Read more

A plant grows.
A river flows.
The body breathes, digests, repairs, adapts and rests.

When movement is available, things can change when and where they need to.

Sometimes that movement becomes restricted.
It may show up as stuckness, procrastination, low energy, burnout, indecision, overthinking, emotional overwhelm, difficulty following through, or the sense that something should be changing, but isn’t.
At other times, movement is already there.
Then the work is to build more strength, range or capacity for what comes next.

Understanding these two principles gives us a way to see where something is strained, stuck or asking for more capacity.

The Wheel of Life

What we see in nature and the body also plays out in how we live. What happens in one area can shape the rest.

The Wheel gives us a simple way to see the whole before deciding where to begin.

Read more

A wheel brings both principles into one simple image.

Its spokes support one another, and together they determine how smoothly it turns. Our own lives work in much the same way.

I use an adaptation of the Wheel of Life, a coaching framework commonly associated with Paul J. Meyer.

Health · Relationships · Family · Work · Home · Money · Purpose · Community · Enjoyment

These are simply useful lenses for looking at the whole. You may name or group them differently.

The invitation is to notice:

  • What is flowing well?
  • Where is there strain?
  • Where is something asking for attention?

Strain travels across the wheel. So can recovery.

The wheel helps us see where something is happening (or not). The next question is: what is underneath it?

What is underneath it?

Movement can begin through four places: the body, mind, emotion and action.

Body

Rest · Resourcing · Strength · Regulation

Mind

Clarity · Perspective · Understanding · Reframing

Emotion

Feeling · Expression · Release · Completion

Action

A decision · A boundary · A conversation · A next step

There is no fixed order.

  • Body → EmotionBody can shift emotion.
  • Emotion → MindEmotion can shift perspective.
  • Mind → ActionClarity can make action possible.
  • Action → AllAction can change the rest.

A practice for working with what we find

Once we see where attention is needed, FACE gives us a simple way to work with what we find.

FFocus on what keeps repeating.

Notice the thought, reaction, tension or behaviour that keeps returning.

We cannot change what we cannot see.

AAdmit where we are not free yet.

Acknowledge where the pattern still overrides our intentions or keeps costing us.

Freedom begins with admitting where we are not free.

CConnect with the pattern from a different place.

Return to the feeling, trigger or situation with more steadiness, support or space.

What is met can begin to move.

EEmbody a different response.

Practise what was not available before: pause, speak, choose or act differently.

Practice turns insight into instinct.

Some things shift quickly. Others take repetition and time. A pattern returning does not mean progress is lost.

The more we face, the more we can embrace.

You do not need to know where to begin.

We can work that out together.
