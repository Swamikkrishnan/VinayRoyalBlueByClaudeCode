The Approach

Welcoming all of life, embodied.

Welcoming all of life has a tangible expression: vitality in the body, healthy immune and hormonal function, and a natural capacity for peace, warmth, care and connection.

It also looks like a resilient, antifragile nervous system — steadiness under pressure, quicker recovery, and a growing capacity through challenge.

To understand how I work towards this, it helps to look more closely at life itself. Two qualities shape my exploration of it: interconnection and movement.

One

Life is interconnected

One of the clearest places to observe life is the living human body.

  • The lungs bring in oxygen.
  • The heart circulates it.
  • Digestion provides energy.
  • The nervous system coordinates how we respond.

Each part has its own role — together, they sustain the whole.

When one is strained, the others compensate. If the strain continues, the whole person is affected. The body lives through this interdependence.

Two

Life moves

When life is working well, things can move when and where they need to.

When that movement becomes restricted, it may show up as —

stuckness · procrastination · low energy · burnout · indecision · overthinking · emotional overwhelm · difficulty following through · loss of direction · relationship troubles

At other times, there is no particular restriction. Life is already moving, and the work is to develop more strength, range or capacity for what comes next.

The Wheel of Life

A simple way to see interconnection and movement together

Consider your life as a wheel. Its different areas form the spokes, and every spoke contributes to the movement of the whole.

A wheel can continue rolling with a weakened spoke, but the others begin carrying more of the load. Over time it wears unevenly and becomes less stable. Our lives work in much the same way.

Strain travels across the wheel. So can recovery.

The spokes show us where in life something is happening. Within any spoke, I have found four places where we can work directly to create or restore movement: Body, Mind, Emotion and Action.

The FACE framework

A practice for welcoming more of life

Where do we get caught in an old pattern, react, shut down or struggle to stay with what is happening? FACE is a framework for meeting that edge.

Choose a letter to open it.

F Focus

Focus on what keeps repeating.

You cannot change what you cannot see.

A Admit

Admit where you are not free yet.

Freedom begins with admitting where you are not free.

C Connect

Connect with what is underneath, with enough steadiness to stay.

Protection becomes limitation when it outlives the danger.

E Embody

Embody a different response.

Practice turns insight into instinct.

A pattern returning does not mean the progress is lost. The more we face, the more we can embrace.
